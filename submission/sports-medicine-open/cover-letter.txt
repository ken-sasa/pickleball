Re: Current Opinion submission, “Building Cumulative Science in an Emerging Sport: The Open Pickleball Research Commons”

Dear Editor,

I am pleased to submit the Current Opinion, “Building Cumulative Science in an Emerging Sport: The Open Pickleball Research Commons,” for consideration in Sports Medicine - Open.

The manuscript addresses a contemporary methodological problem in sport science: how a rapidly emerging sport can build interoperable research infrastructure before incompatible conventions become entrenched. Pickleball already has a growing literature spanning injury epidemiology, exercise and health, aging, psychosocial outcomes, biomechanics, and technology. I argue that the next bottleneck is not simply producing more studies, but enabling independently designed studies to be compared, replicated, combined, and reused.

The article proposes the Open Pickleball Research Commons (OPRC): a Living Evidence Map, a candidate Pickleball Research Minimum Data Set (PR-MDS), low-cost field measurement protocols, and future benchmark modules. It is intentionally a Current Opinion rather than a systematic review. The 28-record Evidence Map is explicitly labeled as an AI-assisted, single-coded proof-of-concept seed; it is not presented as an exhaustive census or formal evidence synthesis. The scientific contribution is the infrastructure architecture, its minimal-core/modular design, and the validation gates required before any component can claim stable-standard status.

This focus aligns with Sports Medicine - Open because it concerns research quality across sporting performance, injury, exercise and health, biomechanics, and sport-specific technology, while offering a concrete implementation of collaborative and open-science principles in an emerging sport.

This is a single-author manuscript. I have approved the submitted version and take full responsibility for its content. It has not been published and is not under consideration elsewhere. No funding was received, and I declare no competing interests. No human participants were recruited and no identifiable participant data were used; ethics approval and consent are therefore not applicable.

OpenAI ChatGPT assisted with literature organization, schema-consistent seed coding, and manuscript drafting. This use is disclosed in the manuscript; I reviewed the content and references and remain responsible for accuracy and interpretation. Figure 1 is a deterministic text-based vector flow chart rather than a generative-AI image. Five supplementary files provide the proof-of-concept Evidence Map, coding manual, PR-MDS specification and machine-readable dictionary, and field measurement protocol. A frozen Zenodo archive is prepared for DOI reservation before final portal submission.

Thank you for your consideration.
