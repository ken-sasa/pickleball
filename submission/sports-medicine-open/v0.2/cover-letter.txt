Re: Current Opinion submission, "Standardize Provenance Before Outcomes: A Research Commons for Cumulative Pickleball Science"

Dear Editor,

I am pleased to submit the Current Opinion, "Standardize Provenance Before Outcomes: A Research Commons for Cumulative Pickleball Science," for consideration in Sports Medicine - Open.

The article addresses a contemporary methodological problem in sport science: how a rapidly emerging sport can establish interoperable research infrastructure before incompatible conventions become entrenched. Its central argument is deliberately narrow. Before attempting field-wide harmonization of outcomes, pickleball research should standardize the minimum context needed to interpret studies together - identifiers, provenance, exposure, derivation rules, and version history.

The manuscript proposes the Open Pickleball Research Commons (OPRC), linking a Living Evidence Map, the candidate Pickleball Research Minimum Data Set (PR-MDS), field protocols, and domain benchmarks. It also defines clear boundaries: PR-MDS is not a core outcome set, reporting guideline, certification scheme, or universal protocol. A frozen 28-record map is included only as an AI-assisted, single-coded engineering proof of concept; it is not presented as a systematic review or estimate of the literature's true composition.

This framing matches the editorial form of Current Opinion described by the journal because it evaluates how research processes and open, versioned infrastructure could affect sport-science practice. The manuscript is also falsifiable: components should advance only through independent reuse, low burden, reliable interpretation, multi-site validation, and public change control, and should be revised or retired when those tests fail.

This is a single-author manuscript. I approve the submitted version and accept full responsibility for its content. It has not been published and is not under consideration elsewhere. No specific funding was received, and I declare no competing interests. The article used no human participants or identifiable participant data.

OpenAI ChatGPT assisted with literature organization, schema-constrained seed coding, manuscript structuring, and language revision. This use is disclosed in the manuscript. I reviewed the cited sources, argument, extracted fields, references, and final text. Figure 1 was generated deterministically from author-reviewed vector-diagram code rather than by generative image synthesis.

Five additional files provide the proof-of-concept Evidence Map, coding manual, PR-MDS specification and dictionary, and field measurement protocol.

Thank you for considering the manuscript.
